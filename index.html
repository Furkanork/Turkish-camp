Why these items, and why 80/20

Heritage speakers usually have strong everyday Turkish: home vocabulary, natural pronunciation, solid A2–B1 grammar. The gap that keeps you from sounding fully fluent is narrow and predictable. It sits in the parts of the language you rarely heard at the kitchen table but meet constantly in news, work, books and with older or formal speakers.

The 20% this site drills

  1. Bağlaçlar (connectors) such as oysa, nitekim, kaldı ki, dolayısıyla. A small closed set that structures every argument, email and opinion.
  2. Eylem yapıları (clause structures) such as -dıkça, -dığı halde, -maksızın, -dığı takdirde. They let you build one complex sentence instead of three short ones: the clearest audible marker of B2 → C1.
  3. Eşdizimler (collocations): which verb goes with which noun: önlem almak, dile getirmek, göz ardı etmek, hayata geçirmek. Native-sounding news and work Turkish is built from these fixed pairs.
  4. Hal & edatlar (verb cases and postpositions): -den kaynaklanmak, -e dayanmak, -e ilişkin. Getting the case right is what separates "understandable" from "correct".
  5. Sosyal ve resmî kalıplar (set phrases and register): kolay gelsin, başınız sağ olsun, gereğini arz ederim. Small, used daily, and socially costly when missing.
  6. Soyut kelimeler (abstract vocabulary) from news and work: eğilim, gerekçe, öngörmek, tutarlı.
  7. Yüksek üslup (formal and Ottoman-origin vocabulary): akabinde, istinaden, mezkûr, muğlak. The layer of Turkish used in law, news and official letters, and where heritage speakers usually hit a wall.
  8. Deyimler ve atasözleri (idioms and proverbs): the ones people actually say, up to C2.

Make it hard on purpose. By default the deck releases only C1 and C2 items, and it asks you to type the Turkish from the English meaning and a gapped sentence. Recognising a word is easy for a heritage speaker; producing the exact form is the real test. You can switch to B2+ or to recall mode at the top of the Practise tab.

The Grammar tab covers the structures that do the most work at B2–C1: relative clauses, that-clauses, indirect questions, causative, passive, evidential -mış, conditionals, compound tenses, modality, reported speech, formal written style and the spelling points (de/da, ki, mi) that give heritage writers away.

How the deck uses it

Each category has a weight (the bars on the Practise tab). New cards are released in weighted order, so connectors and clause structures arrive first and idioms last. Reviews use a simple Leitner schedule: Biliyorum moves a card up a box (next review in 1, 3, 7, 16, then 35 days), Zor brings it back tomorrow, Tekrar repeats it in this session.

Getting the other 80% for free

The deck is only the input. Use each new item in the Yazma box, then out loud with family, and read one Turkish opinion column a day: they are dense with exactly these connectors and structures.

The category weights are a teaching judgement based on what heritage learners typically lack, not a measured corpus statistic. Progress is saved in this browser only.
